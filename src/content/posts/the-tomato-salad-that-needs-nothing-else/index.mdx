There is a version of this salad on every restaurant menu between June and September, and most of them have too much in them. Shallots, capers, a mustard vinaigrette, a hard cheese — each one is defensible on its own and together they bury the thing you paid for.

A tomato at the height of its season is already sweet, already acidic, and already savoury. What it wants is salt, fat, and time. Everything else is a hedge against tomatoes that are not very good, and the honest answer in that case is to cook them instead.

## Why the waiting matters

Salt does not season the surface of a tomato so much as it opens it. Twenty minutes after salting, there is a slick of pink juice on the platter that was not there before, and it is more concentrated than anything you could have made in a bowl. Pour the oil in and it emulsifies on contact.

This is also why the salad is assembled on the plate it is served from. Tossed in a bowl, the juice stays in the bowl.

## What to put beside it

Bread, torn not sliced. A hard-boiled egg if it is lunch. Cold white wine, or a beer, and nothing that needs a knife.

If you want to make it a meal, [the focaccia](/post/no-knead-focaccia-with-too-much-olive-oil/) is the right bread, and you can tear it straight into the juice.
