Meal planning usually fails for the same reason diets do: it demands that Thursday-evening you behave like Sunday-morning you. Six recipes chosen on Sunday will be six recipes you do not feel like cooking by Wednesday, and then the vegetables rot and the whole thing feels like a failure.

The approach that has actually worked for me is to plan the shop, not the week. Buy a set of ingredients that overlap heavily, and decide what to cook on the night.

## The overlap principle

Pick a handful of building blocks that appear in a lot of dishes, and buy those in quantity. Then buy two or three things that only go one way.

A week's version of that looks like this:

**Everything week:** olive oil, onions, garlic, lemons, tinned tomatoes, a bag of dried or two tins of beans, parmesan, eggs, bread, yogurt, and a large bunch each of parsley and dill.

**This week specifically:** a whole chicken, 800g of carrots, a cabbage, a punnet of tomatoes, 500g of pasta, and a bag of rice.

That is one shop, no exotic single-use purchases, and everything on it goes at least two ways.

## Where it leads

**Sunday.** [Roast the chicken over torn potatoes.](/post/roast-chicken-over-torn-potatoes/) Eat half. Keep the carcass.

**Monday.** Chicken stock from the carcass while you make [the bean stew](/post/white-bean-stew-with-lemon-and-dill/) — the dill and the lemon are already in.

**Tuesday.** [Tomato butter pasta.](/post/tomato-butter-pasta-in-fifteen-minutes/) Fifteen minutes, tins and an onion. Make double sauce.

**Wednesday.** The rest of the chicken, shredded, over rice, with [charred cabbage](/post/charred-cabbage-with-brown-butter/) on the side.

**Thursday.** [Roast carrots with yogurt](/post/roast-carrots-with-yogurt-and-dukkah/), and the leftover rice fried [into a crust with eggs](/post/crispy-rice-with-fried-eggs-and-chili-oil/).

**Friday.** [Tomato salad](/post/the-tomato-salad-that-needs-nothing-else/), bread, the second half of Tuesday's sauce baked with pasta and parmesan, and nobody cooks properly on a Friday.

Six dinners, one shop, and no night where the plan was already decided for you.

## Two rules that make it work

**Cook once, eat twice, deliberately.** Not "leftovers" as an accident, but a batch made on purpose: double the pasta sauce, cook the whole bag of beans, roast two trays of carrots instead of one. The second meal has to be a different dish, not the same plate again — that is the part that keeps it from feeling like penance.

**Buy herbs in bunches and use them like vegetables.** A supermarket packet of six dill sprigs is decoration. A large bunch, used by the fistful, is an ingredient — and a bunch of parsley and a bunch of dill between them will carry a week of otherwise unrelated dishes and make them taste intentional.

## What to do with the last two carrots

Nothing formal. Grate them into a bowl with lemon juice, olive oil, salt, and whatever herbs are left, and eat that beside anything. Every good cook I know has a version of this, and none of them would call it a recipe.
