The compound butter under the skin is a restaurant move and it takes ninety seconds. Everything else about this is a traybake, which is to say the oven does it and you do not.

The reason to bother with the pocket is temperature. Anything sugary — honey, gochujang, miso, most marinades — sitting on the surface of chicken at 220C is going to be black before the thigh is cooked. Tucked underneath, the same mixture is protected by the skin, melts into the meat as the fat renders, and seasons from the inside.

## What the skin is for

Left dry and salted, chicken skin at high heat renders its own fat and crisps. That fat drips onto the potatoes below, which is the actual argument for putting them in the same tray: they fry in schmaltz rather than roasting in oil.

Which is also why nothing goes on top of the skin except salt. Not oil, not more butter, not the marinade.

## The potato layer

Halve them and lay them cut-side down. They want contact with hot metal for the same reason the broccoli did — a flat face browns, a curve does not. Onion wedges go in among them to sweeten and half-collapse.

Anything with more water than a potato will ruin the tray. Courgettes, peppers, and tomatoes all belong in a different recipe.

## Knowing when it is done

Seventy-five degrees at the thickest part, next to the bone but not touching it. Thighs are forgiving — five degrees over will not hurt them — but the potatoes underneath are the real clock, and if they are not tender the chicken has not been in long enough.
