This is the dish I make when people are coming and I have not planned anything, and it has never once failed to be the thing that gets finished first. It takes ten minutes and three of them are the food processor running.

## Why it needs the full two minutes

Feta is a curd cheese with a crumbly, granular structure. Blitzed briefly it becomes a paste of smaller crumbs; blitzed properly, with fat and acid present, the protein network breaks down and reassembles around the oil and yogurt into something aerated and genuinely light. The transition is sudden and it happens well after the point you would assume it was done.

Stop at forty seconds and you have made a slightly wet feta. Run it to two minutes and you have made something that looks like it came from a restaurant.

## Brine matters

Feta sold in a block in brine has been kept wet and has nothing added to it. Pre-crumbled feta is dusted with potato or cellulose starch to keep the pieces apart, and that starch is why it goes claggy rather than smooth in a processor. It is the single most common reason this recipe disappoints people.

## Hot honey, quickly

Honey held over heat for more than a minute darkens and starts to taste of caramel rather than of honey, and the chilli goes bitter. Thirty seconds is enough to loosen it and bloom the chilli oil. The vinegar off the heat is what makes it a dressing rather than a syrup.

## Ridges, not a dome

Spread it wide and drag a spoon through it. Honey poured over a smooth mound runs off to the edges; poured into a landscape of ridges and wells it stays where it lands, and every scoop of bread gets both.

Warm flatbread, torn [focaccia](/post/no-knead-focaccia-with-too-much-olive-oil/), or raw radishes and cucumber if you would rather not have bread at all.
