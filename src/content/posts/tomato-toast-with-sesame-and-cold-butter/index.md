A Catalan _pa amb tomàquet_ has bread, tomato, oil, and salt, and it is one of the great things you can eat. This is a northern-European corruption of it with butter in place of some of the oil, and it is the thing I make most often for lunch when there is nothing in the house.

## Cold butter, in slices

The instinct is to spread. Do not. Cut the butter into thin slabs with a knife and lay them on the toast — the underside melts into the hot crumb and seals it, while the top of each slice stays cool and solid. When you bite through, you get warm buttered bread and cold salted butter in the same mouthful, and it is much better than either alone.

This only works with genuinely cold butter, so it comes out of the fridge at the last second.

## Salting the tomato first

Two minutes with salt on it does two things: it seasons the tomato all the way through rather than only on the surface, and it draws out a little juice which then gets poured back over. The same principle as [the tomato salad](/post/the-tomato-salad-that-needs-nothing-else/), on a smaller scale and a shorter clock.

## Sesame, toasted

Sesame is here for texture and for a nutty note that flatters the tomato in a way that basil, the obvious choice, does not. But it has to be toasted — raw sesame is faintly bitter and contributes almost nothing.

Two minutes in a dry pan, moving constantly, until they colour and start to pop.
