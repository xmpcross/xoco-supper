Everything on this site takes longer than this and almost nothing gets eaten faster. It is also the only recipe here that is genuinely better made in advance.

## Infusing is not frying

The temptation with a pan of oil and aromatics is to hear something. Resist it. Above about 60C, rosemary turns medicinal, garlic starts to brown and go bitter, and the orange peel loses its top notes. Eight minutes on the lowest flame your hob will hold extracts the fat-soluble aromatics without cooking any of them.

If it sizzles, take it off, let it cool a little, and start again lower.

## Orange peel, pared not zested

Cut strips with a knife or a peeler and check the backs for white pith, which is bitter. The oil in the coloured layer is what you want, and it is the ingredient that makes a bowl of supermarket olives taste like they came from somewhere.

## Two hours minimum, three days better

The olives are already cured and salted; what the marinade does is displace some of that brine at the surface with flavoured oil, and that takes time. Two hours is noticeable. Overnight is much better. Three days is the point at which people ask where you bought them.

Room temperature throughout. The fridge is the enemy of this dish.

## The almonds, and toasting them dry

Nuts toasted in oil from cold fry unevenly and the oil takes on a slightly stale note before the centres are done. Toasted dry, they roast from the inside, and the oil goes on at the end purely as a vehicle for the salt and paprika.

They should be warm when they reach the table. It is a small thing and it makes an ordinary bowl of nuts into something somebody comments on.
