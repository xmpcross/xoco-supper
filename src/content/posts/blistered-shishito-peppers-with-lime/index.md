The shortest recipe on this site, and one of the two or three I make most often. It is what to cook when people arrive and you want something on the table in the time it takes to open a bottle.

## The pan has to be genuinely too hot

Blistering is not roasting. You want the thin skin of the pepper to char and lift away from the flesh underneath in patches, which happens fast and only at high temperature. A moderate pan cooks the peppers through evenly and gives you something soft and green and dull.

Cast iron holds the heat best, because three hundred grams of cold peppers will drop the temperature of a thin pan the moment they land.

## Neutral oil, not olive

Olive oil's smoke point is well below where this needs to happen, and it will fill the kitchen with acrid smoke and taste of it. Groundnut, rapeseed, or sunflower.

## Salt while the oil is hot

Salt sticks to hot oil and slides off cool peppers. Tip them into the bowl and salt them immediately, tossing, and use more than you think is reasonable — these are eaten with fingers, one at a time, and each one needs to carry its own seasoning.

## The lottery

Shishitos and padróns are mild cultivars that occasionally, unpredictably, produce a fruit with real capsaicin in it — usually reckoned at about one in ten, though it varies with the season and how stressed the plants were. There is no visual tell.

Serve them with a cold drink and let people find out.
