Steamed broccoli is a vegetable you have to be talked into. Charred broccoli is one you eat standing at the stove before it reaches the table, and the difference is entirely about heat and patience.

Most of the sweetness in a brassica is locked behind a sulphurous edge that boiling makes worse and browning removes. High, dry heat on a cut face converts sugars, drives off water, and leaves something closer to roasted cauliflower than to school dinners.

## The flat face

Broccoli is an awkward shape and the trick is to make it less awkward. Cutting wedges through the stem, rather than breaking off florets, gives every piece a flat plane of dense stem to sit on. That plane is what browns. Loose florets have almost no surface that can contact the pan, which is why a tray of them comes out dusty and dry.

Peel the main stalk and slice it into coins. It cooks in the same time and it is better than the florets.

## Anchovy as seasoning, not ingredient

Four fillets in a pan of butter melt into nothing visible in about sixty seconds. What is left is glutamate and salt — the same reason a Caesar dressing tastes the way it does without anybody thinking about fish.

If you are cooking for somebody who has told you they do not like anchovies, this is the dish to make them, and I would not mention it until afterwards.

## Turning it into dinner

With bread and a fried egg it is a meal for two. With [the crispy rice](/post/crispy-rice-with-fried-eggs-and-chili-oil/) it is a better one. As a side it stretches to four and will still be the thing people mention.
