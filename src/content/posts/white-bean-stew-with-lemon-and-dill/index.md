Tinned beans have a reputation problem: they are what you eat when you have not shopped. But a tin of beans plus four tablespoons of olive oil and a whole lemon is not a compromise, it is a Mediterranean dish that happens to take twenty minutes.

The thing that makes it work is the same thing that makes most bean dishes work — texture contrast between whole beans and crushed ones — and the thing that makes it taste like food rather than like a tin is finishing with acid and raw oil off the heat.

## Crushing a third

Beans suspended in stock are a soup. Beans suspended in their own starch are a stew, and the difference is a spoon pressed against the side of the pan for thirty seconds. About a third is right: enough to thicken, not so much that everything turns to purée.

This is also why the tin liquid stays. Rinsing beans washes away exactly the starch you are trying to recruit.

## Lemon off the heat

Citric acid is volatile and heat-sensitive. Squeeze lemon into a simmering pan and by the time it reaches the table it tastes generically sour rather than specifically of lemon. Off the heat, thirty seconds before serving, it stays sharp and floral.

Dill has the same problem, worse. It should barely be warm.

## Fat as seasoning

Four tablespoons in the pan, then more raw over the top. The cooked oil carries the fennel and garlic through the dish; the raw oil sits on the surface and hits first. If you have a peppery oil, this is the dish to open it for — and if you are not sure what you have, [that is a longer conversation](/post/how-to-buy-olive-oil-without-being-fooled/).

## What goes with it

Bread, and something bitter and green. It is also very good under a fried egg the next morning.
