Two ingredients and a spice, and it is the pudding I make more than any other between May and August. It relies on two pieces of kitchen chemistry that both sound like affectations and are not.

## Maceration

Sugar on cut fruit draws water out by osmosis. Within half an hour the strawberries have released a syrup that is more intensely strawberry than the fruit was, because it is the fruit's own juice with sugar dissolved in it and none of the water diluting it. The fruit itself firms up and concentrates.

The tablespoon of balsamic is doing what the lemon does elsewhere: strawberries at their best are very sweet and quite low in acid, and without something sharp the whole bowl reads as one flat note.

## The salt

A pinch, in the fruit, not the cream. Salt suppresses bitterness and raises the perceived sweetness and aroma of fruit — it is the reason salted watermelon and salted mango exist. Leave it out and this is noticeably duller, in a way that is hard to place until you do it both ways side by side.

## Black pepper is not a joke

Strawberry and black pepper share several terpenes, and pepper's own aromatics are floral and slightly resinous before they are hot. Cracked coarse and folded into cream, the effect is not "peppery pudding" — it is a strawberry that seems more like itself, with a warm catch at the back.

Half a teaspoon for 300ml of cream. Coarse, so it turns up as an occasional pop rather than a haze.

## Soft peaks

Cream whipped to stiff peaks is structural — for piping, for holding a shape. Here it is a sauce, and it should slump. Stop when the whisk leaves a trail that closes slowly.
