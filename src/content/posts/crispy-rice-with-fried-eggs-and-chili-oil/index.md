Every rice-eating culture has a word for the crust at the bottom of the pot, which tells you it was never an accident. Persian _tahdig_, Korean _nurungji_, Spanish _socarrat_ — the same texture, arrived at independently, and always the part people fight over.

This is the lazy domestic version: no pot, no inversion, no ceremony. Cold rice, a wide pan, and the discipline to leave it alone.

## The discipline part

There is exactly one way to fail at this, and it is stirring. Rice crisps because a layer of it stays in continuous contact with hot metal long enough for the surface starch to dehydrate and brown. Every stir resets that clock.

Six to eight minutes of doing nothing feels much longer than it is. Set a timer, walk away, and prepare the eggs while you wait.

## Why cold rice

Refrigeration retrogrades the starch: the molecules recrystallise and the grains firm up and dry out slightly. That firmness is what lets them hold a shape under a spatula, and the lower surface moisture is what lets them brown rather than steam.

Fresh rice, pressed into a hot pan, releases water, and water at 100C cannot brown anything.

## The eggs are a delivery mechanism

Fried hard and fast in a good amount of oil, so the edges go lacy and brown and the yolk stays liquid. That yolk is the sauce — it runs into the broken crust and turns half of it soft while the other half stays crunchy, which is the actual pleasure of the dish.

## What it is really for

This is what you cook on a Tuesday when the fridge has one container of rice and four eggs in it, and it is better than that sentence suggests. Add [the charred broccoli](/post/charred-broccoli-with-anchovy-butter/) on the side and it is a dinner you would serve someone.
